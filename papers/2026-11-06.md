<p><strong>相关性：</strong>匹配关注项：致密双星建模、LVK/LISA/引力波观测、QNM/黑洞扰动。 这是 gr-qc 每日新增或更新条目。</p>

<details>
<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>The uncertain calibration of gravitational wave detectors is one source of systematic error in gravitational wave inference. While generally small, this uncertainty can be accounted for by modeling and marginalizing over calibration corrections. In this work we extend the standard frequency-domain approach to calibration marginalization to the time domain using basis splines, in such a way that the marginalization is analytic. We apply this method to the time-domain analysis of black hole ringdown, in particular the two highest signal-to-noise events detected so far, GW250114 and GW250207. For GW250114 we continue to find strong evidence for two ringdown modes, after marginalizing over the small calibration uncertainty at the time of the event. For GW250207, the calibration of the LIGO-Hanford detector was uncertain, and calibration marginalization is required for a coherent analysis using both LIGO detectors. We find that when calibration effects are included the remnant mass and spins inferred during ringdown agree with inferences from the full signal. Despite the high signal-to-noise of GW250207, we find no evidence for the presence of a second mode in the ringdown, and as a result deviations from relativity during ringdown are not well constrained.</p>

<p>We present a Transformer model for merger-centered gravitational waveforms from precessing binary black holes. <p>Gravitational waves from compact binary coalescences provide a unique opportunity to test general relativity in the strong-field regime. <p>Gravitational wave observations of neutron star mergers provide a unique window into the interiors of neutron stars, probing dense matter from microscopic nuclear interactions to the macroscopic structure, including their maximum mass, beyond which the remnant collapses to a black hole. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Gravitational waves from binary neutron star mergers encode the properties of the densest matter in the universe, but recovering them accurately requires controlling biases introduced by the inference framework. <p>Detecting single gravitons is possible through the extraction of discrete energy quanta from passing gravitational waves. For decades, this was considered hopeless, with estimates suggesting a detector the size of Jupiter. Here, I explain how these earlier estimates relied on the highly inefficient electron ionization process, while the absorption through the full mass quadrupole of a macroscopic system can bring graviton detection within experimental reach. Operating in the LIGO frequency band solves the challenges of graviton sources and noise suppression, allowing gravitons to be extracted from known passing gravitational waves and detection events to be heralded by classical gravitational-wave observations. Combined with the ability to resolve single energy quanta in macroscopic systems, such as in quantum acoustics, this opens a realistic path toward graviton detection. I also discuss the general limitations of particle detection in establishing an underlying quantum theory, illustrated by an energy-nonconserving semiclassical alternative. This opens the way not only to the first detection of individual gravitons, but also to experimentally probing linearized quantum gravity and the graviton counting statistics of gravitational waves.</p>
