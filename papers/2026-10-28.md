<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Gravitational-wave (GW) observations of binary black holes provide a powerful probe of general relativity (GR) in the strong-field regime. In effective field theory (EFT) extensions of GR, higher-curvature operators modify the binary dynamics and introduce corrections to the inspiral phase. In this work, we consider two parity-even cubic-curvature couplings and one quartic-curvature coupling, whose leading phase corrections enter at 5PN, 6PN, and 8PN order, respectively. We incorporate these corrections into the individual multipoles of the \texttt{SEOBNRv5HM\_ROM} waveform using the flexible theory-independent framework, allowing higher-order modes and the full inspiral-merger-ringdown signal to be used in the analysis. We first perform a Fisher-matrix analysis of 92 binary-black-hole events selected from a parent sample of 273 events in the public LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA catalogs. GW230627\_015337 provides the tightest forecast constraints on all three EFT couplings and is therefore selected for a full Bayesian analysis. The posterior distributions are consistent with GR, with all three couplings consistent with zero at 90\% credibility. The corresponding one-sided 90\% upper limits on the characteristic length scales are \(17.1\), \(11.1\), and \(18.3\,\mathrm{km}\). The log Bayes factors of the three EFT models relative to GR range from \(-5.49\) to \(-2.51\), favoring GR under the adopted priors. These bounds probe higher-curvature length scales at the level of \(10\)--\(20\,\mathrm{km}\) and are numerically tighter than representative previous inspiral and ringdown constraints, although differences in the models and analysis assumptions should be taken into account.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Modeling the gravitational waves from merging black holes requires precise calculations of the two-body problem. The post-Minkowskian expansion of black-hole scattering, in powers of Newton&#x27;s constant, now stands at fifth order, four loops. At this order one part of the deflection diverges when the black holes pass at about 94 percent of the speed of light. The divergence is known to come from periods of a K3 surface, which has a node at this speed. We show that the node lies on the integration contour of the K3 maximal cut, which makes this speed a Landau singularity of the cut. Requiring the divergence to cancel, without introducing new infinities, has been shown to fix the memory contribution, named for the permanent displacement that the waves leave in test masses, up to one constant. We derive the leading coefficients of its boundary integrals in closed form. We obtain both results from maximal cuts and boundary limits, without an integration-by-parts reduction of the full four-loop integrand. We also continue the K3 and Calabi--Yau periods of these integrals to the energies of bound orbits, as in the inspiral before a merger.</p>

<p>Standard sirens provide a direct probe of the cosmic distance-redshift relation and therefore a powerful test of whether gravitational waves (GWs) propagate as predicted by general relativity on cosmological scales. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>In warm inflation, the primordial fluctuations are driven by random kicks from a hot radiation bath. Two features of these kicks matter: how long the bath remembers them, which shapes the power spectrum, and how their strengths are spread, which controls the rare, extreme fluctuations. In this paper, we investigate how primordial black holes and the accompanying induced gravitational waves can be used to discriminate between these two features. We use a simple model inspired by warm inflation, in which independent kicks of random strength add up to a narrow peak in the power spectrum.
