<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>A weak longitudinal background can imitate the angular response of a gravitational-wave propagation mass. We quantify this ambiguity in a compressed Gaussian experiment constructed from the NANOGrav 15-year timing data. At a false-alarm probability of $10^{-3}$, allowing a weak longitudinal component in a massless background costs at least 16.29 percentage points of optimal rejection power for a specified source in a 20-pulsar subarray. Expanding the array to 40 pulsars at the same background level recovers at least 7.62 points. In a separate low-background experiment, we allow the same continuous range of longitudinal amplitudes at zero and positive mass. Across that range and three injected background levels, one test rejects zero mass with at least 97.38\% power at either of two masses separated by 1.08\%. With false assignment limited to $10^{-3}$ at the other mass, the power to identify either mass is at most 1.584\%. A cutoff can thus be identifiable before its position within a frequency cell can be resolved.</p>

<p><strong>相关性：</strong>匹配关注项：致密双星建模、LVK/LISA/引力波观测。</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>In hierarchical galaxy formation, supermassive black hole binaries are a ubiquitous consequence of frequent galaxy mergers, yet subparsec binaries remain difficult to be confirmed. Periodic flux modulations may arise from the orbital motion of black holes in close binaries, while relativistic jet precession can provide an additional source of periodic variability. Such periodic signatures therefore motivate us to identify close binary candidates. Here, we report an optical periodicity of $269^{+13}_{-14}$ days in both Zwicky Transient Facility g and r bands of the blazar PKS 0301-243, with corrected global significances of $2.75σ$ and $2.18σ$, respectively. This signal persists over 7 cycles across a baseline of ~2000 days. We interpret this period as a possible modulation associated with the orbital motion of a supermassive black hole binary. A longer optical periodicity of $889^{+47}_{-46}$ days is identified in Katzman Automatic Imaging Telescope, with corrected global significance of $1.97σ$. The longer periodicity spans approximately 5 cycles over a baseline of ~4500 days, potentially associated with jet precession. Both periodicities are independently identified using multiple methods. A supermassive black hole binary incorporating a relativistic jet and a circumbinary disc could reproduce the observed light curve ensemble. For a total mass of $10^{8.95} M_\odot$ and a mass ratio q = 0.1, the derived milli-parsec separation and a short gravitational wave driven coalescence time, $t_c = 1122^{+144}_{-144}$ years, imply that PKS 0301-243 may be a rare source of nano-hertz gravitational waves and a potential multimessenger laboratory for the terminal stages of supermassive black hole coalescence.</p>

<p>Federated learning servers combine client models with a fixed aggregation rule, and each rule in common use is designed for one kind of heterogeneity. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Quantization reduces the memory and bandwidth demands of large language model (LLM) inference but can degrade accuracy; offloading higher-precision data can instead stall generation.
