<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>This thesis investigates the perturbative dynamics and phenomenology of compact objects in two approaches to quantum gravity: quadratic gravity and asymptotic safety. Linear perturbations provide a tool to probe the physical properties of spacetimes and their potential observational signatures, including quasinormal modes, black hole shadows, and Hawking radiation. The first part focuses on Einstein-Weyl gravity, where an additional massive spin-2 degree of freedom modifies the dynamics of compact objects. We derive and analyze tensor perturbation for static, spherically symmetric solutions, identifying Gregory-Laflamme-type instabilities in the monopole sector and studying the time domain evolution of higher multipoles. We also investigate universal massive-field tails and the breakdown of the standard eikonal correspondence between quasinormal modes and null geodesics. Particular attention is devoted to veiled singularities, horizonless configurations that satisfy a form of weak cosmic censorship and can mimic black hole shadows. The second part examines regular black holes in asymptotically safe gravity. Using the proper-time flow approach, we construct and investigate a dynamical regular black hole solution arising from gravitational collapse. We analyze its perturbative stability and quasinormal mode spectrum, finding characteristic deviations from Schwarzschild black holes. We explore the phenomenological implications of quantum corrections for shadows and Hawking radiation. While shadow remains compatible with current constraints, the modified evaporation process features lower temperatures and suppressed emission rates, with implications for primordial black holes and their possible contribution to dark matter. Overall, this work demonstrates how the perturbative dynamics of compact objects can connect quantum gravity models to potentially observable astrophysical phenomena.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Recently, the Five-hundred-meter-Aperture-Spherical-radio-Telescope (FAST) Galactic Plane Pulsar Snapshot (GPPS) survey discovers an extraordinary binary neutron star (BNS) system, PSR J0653+0443, possessing the highest known orbital eccentricity ($e\approx0.992$) among Galactic BNSs (Yang et al., 2026), which provides a unique probe into the formation and evolution of BNSs. We find that BNSs with $e&gt;0.99$ constitute only $\sim0.6\%$ of the detectable population, with an expected detection number of $λ_{\mathrm{GPPS}}\approx0.31$ over the full planned footprint. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>In this manuscript, we study circular geodesics and twin-peak high-frequency quasi-periodic oscillations (HF-QPOs) in the charged Simpson--Visser--anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetime, which depends on a charge parameter $Q$, a regularization scale $b$ and an AdS radius $l$. Written in terms of the areal radius $R=\sqrt{r^{2}+b^{2}}$, the specific energy, angular momentum, Keplerian frequency and innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) of test particles coincide with those of the Reissner--Nordström--AdS black hole, so $b$ enters the epicyclic spectrum only through a factor $1-b^{2}/R^{2}$ in the radial frequency. We give this frequency and the ISCO condition in closed form. We then fit the relativistic-precession model to the HF-QPO pairs of GRO~J1655$-$40 and XTE~J1550$-$564, with their dynamical masses included as Gaussian priors. The Schwarzschild limit gives $χ^{2}=32.9$ for two degrees of freedom. One additional deformation removes this tension: $Q/M=0.814^{+0.059}_{-0.074}$ at fixed $l=100M$, or $l=40.6^{+6.2}_{-4.8}\,M$ at $Q=0$, both with $χ^{2}\simeq0.13$. These two solutions lie on a single degeneracy valley in the $(Q,l)$ plane, and, at $l=100M$, $b$ is left unconstrained up to $b\simeq3.9M$ (68\%), a limit set by the charge prior rather than by the data. A Kerr black hole with spin $a\simeq0.3$--$0.4$ reproduces the same pairs, so the inferred deformation most likely absorbs the spin neglected by the static metric. Our results therefore describe degeneracies among orbital-frequency deformations; they are not a measurement of an electric charge or of a cosmological constant.</p>
</details>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Sequence compression is a fundamental operation and a critical information bottleneck in efficient language and multimodal models, and yet prevailing approaches based on pooling or learned projections remain underperforming. We revisit sequence compression through the lens of test-time regression: input tokens define a regression problem whose fitted coefficients form the compressed representation. Importantly, the regression problem is learned end-to-end via backpropagation, while its coefficients are computed efficiently in closed form during the forward pass. This leads to TTR-Compressor, a general alternative to conventional pooling and projection-based compression. We evaluate TTR-Compressor as the compressor for block-wise sparse attention and the visual token merger for multimodal large language models. On block-wise sparse attention, TTR-Compressor improves RULER performance over attention-based compressors by a large margin. For visual token merging, it consistently outperforms the standard MLP merger and matches its performance with 55.6% fewer visual tokens. These results establish test-time regression as an effective primitive for sequence compression in efficient language and multimodal models.</p>

<p>Purpose: To describe an evidence-aware retrieval evaluation protocol for a clinical retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system over breast lesion management literature, addressing the dependence of retrieval scores on annotation coverage.
