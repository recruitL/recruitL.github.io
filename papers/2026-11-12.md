<p>In this paper, we have generalized Poisson&#x27;s(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. Focusing on the 22-mode, we have found a novel 0.5PN order term beyond the quadrupole order, which vanishes in the black hole limit. Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).</p>

<details>
<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Black holes provide a privileged window on gravity in the strong-field regime, and deviations from the classical solutions of General Relativity (GR) may encode information about quantum gravity and other extensions of the theory. The abundance of proposed deformed black hole geometries, however, makes model-by-model comparison with observations impractical. This review presents a unified account of the Effective Metric Description (EMD), a model-independent framework in which deformations of static, spherically symmetric black hole spacetimes are parametrized by physical, coordinate-invariant quantities, such as the proper radial distance from the event horizon or the proper time of a free-falling observer. After reviewing the classical black hole solutions and the open issues that motivate going beyond GR, we develop the EMD as a series expansion near the horizon for a given physical quantity. We provide the general solution of the resulting self-consistency conditions compatible with the symmetries of the geometry, derive universal regularity conditions ensuring a finite Hawking temperature and curvature invariants, establish the equivalence of descriptions based on different physical quantities, and extend the framework to charged, extremal and lower-dimensional black holes. Padé resummation extends the description away from the horizon, enabling model-independent computations of the black hole shadow and, in the eikonal limit, of quasi-normal mode frequencies in the test-field approximation. The framework is tested on a broad set of geometries used to model physics beyond GR, and compared with other model-independent parametrizations through explicit transformation maps. As an effective description of deformations beyond GR, the EMD provides a coherent bridge between fundamental black hole physics, quantum-gravity-inspired deformations, and astrophysical observables.</p>

<p>Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals (EMRIs) are key targets for the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna, but their long, multi-harmonic waveforms make search and parameter estimation computationally expensive. Frequency-track-based SBI thus provides a fast, waveform-free way to identify EMRI harmonics and obtain tight prior volumes for targeted follow-up, substantially reducing the burden on subsequent MCMC-based analyses.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>The Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff equations admit singular solutions in which a negative point mass is surrounded by a self-gravitating fluid. For a $Γ$-law polytropic equation of state, these solutions, which we call singustars, are thick shells bounded by two zero-pressure surfaces around a naked, repulsive, negative-mass singularity, with a Schwarzschild exterior. They form a two-parameter family, labeled by the mass $M$ and the compactness $C\equiv M/R$. Singustars join regular polytropes as the central negative mass vanishes and reach compactness beyond the Buchdahl bound. Nonlinear evolutions in ingoing null coordinates show that the end state depends on the perturbation. For the collapsing models studied, backward tracing of outgoing null rays identifies an event-horizon generator with a past endpoint on the singularity, before the first trapped surface appears. Outward perturbations disperse unbound shells and leave the singularity naked, while a bound shell migrates toward, and oscillates about, a stable configuration with the same conserved masses.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>LLM agents are increasingly used for open-ended, data-driven scientific discovery, yet most of them evaluate hypotheses pointwise, testing an empirical claim $h$ only against its complement $\neg h$. When evidence rejects $h$, the agent learns that one explanation is unlikely but not which alternative the evidence supports. To recover that alternative, we introduce Contrastive Discovery, which replaces the single hypothesis with a contrast set, a scientific question paired with mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive competing explanations. Each experiment is scored by how its evidence redistributes belief across the set, and the question is resolved when belief concentrates on one explanation. Because contrast sets change only the unit an agent proposes and scores, an existing agent can use them to re-evaluate evidence it has already collected or to guide its search. Across six domains, $89\%$ of the pointwise candidates with the largest belief shifts are rejections. On the same evidence, contrast sets resolve $73\%$ of these candidates to a specific alternative. When contrast sets guide search, the agent resolves $67\%$ more questions than pointwise surprisal under the same experiment budget. In a blinded study, $38$ human experts chose the same explanation on $78\%$ of resolved questions and on $82\%$ of questions where the pointwise agent rejected its hypothesis and stopped. Together, these results support explicit competing explanations as a way for discovery agents to resolve more scientific questions from the same evidence and validation budget.</p>
