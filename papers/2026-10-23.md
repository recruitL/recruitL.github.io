<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>The waveform left after quasinormal modes filtering of binary gravitational waveforms, often called the direct wave, has recently attracted considerable debate. Analytic models of this residual waveform involve several levels of approximation, while the effects of filtering on the modeling remain unclear. These issues require further investigation. In this paper, we examine the approximation errors, limits of applicability, and role of filtering in these models. We find that the point-particle models examined here show notable discrepancies with the filtered Binary-black-holes waveforms. Analytic particle templates based on the stationary-phase approximation perform reasonably well for the \((\ell,m)=(2,2)\) multipole, but exhibit substantial limitations for the \((4,4)\). Although the filter is all-pass, it substantially affects the modeling results. Our analytic calculations on a Schwarzschild background further show that vacuum perturbations with Gaussian initial data can also exhibit direct-wave-like waveforms after filtering, with their morphology depending on the location of the initial data relative to the light ring.</p>

<p>I apply and extend the formalism of osculating orbits of Damour et al. (2004) and Lynch et al. <p>Parallel AI agents can produce useful local edits yet degrade a shared solution when their edits are combined. We model this coordination problem as discrete block optimization with an exactly evaluable objective and a known dependency graph. Independent sets certify compatible edits whose verified gains, measured against the same current solution, add exactly. We analytically continue the gravitational amplitudes computed from these triangulations to de Sitter parameters and use them to infer OPE correlations. We recover matching normalized amplitudes from a complex Liouville cosmological wavefunction. A non-Gaussian fourth moment encodes the hyperbolic core volume in an oscillatory semiclassical phase. We construct the associated Lorentzian de Sitter spacetime and show that derivatives of this phase recover its boost holonomies. Its spacelike future boundary carries a positive-definite conformal metric, while the wavefunction obeys Euclidean conformal Ward identities. These relations connect the proposed OPE statistics to both the Euclidean boundary description and the Lorentzian bulk geometry. Integrating the absolute square of the cosmological wavefunction over boundary moduli gives cosmological observables. Their long-collar factorization reproduces the edge measure of the two-matrix model.</p>
