<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Matter around black holes affects the ringdown in two ways: through the background geometry and through the response of the matter itself. We compute axial and polar gravitational quasinormal modes of a black hole embedded in a relativistic Hernquist-type dark matter halo, following the fundamental mode and the first three overtones associated with the vacuum Schwarzschild spectrum. The polar sector couples metric and matter perturbations. At small halo compactness, both sectors follow the familiar gravitational redshift law. Most of the departure from the typical redshift at larger compactness is accounted for by the exact horizon lapse factor of the background. The remaining residual departure to this lapse is sector dependent. Axial and polar frequencies are split, with their splitting growing from the fundamental through the third overtone. At large halo compactness the split grows by an order of magnitude, while overtone-to-fundamental frequency ratios depart from a common rescaling. Within the adopted dark matter profile, the halo acts on the ringdown as a prism that disperses the gravitational spectrum, leaving a parity-dependent imprint that no common rescaling of the frequencies can reproduce. Parity-isospectrality is, thus, broken.</p>

<p>Extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) offer a promising probe of matter environments around massive black holes through gravitational-wave (GW) observations. We investigate orbital resonances induced by conservative gravitational perturbations from such environments. Combining a multiparameter expansion with the action-angle formalism, we derive a general resonance condition for generic bound Kerr orbits. Applying this framework to ultralight scalar clouds formed through black hole superradiance, we show that orbital resonances can arise from a single-mode cloud without requiring transitions between cloud modes. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>The surface impedance of a body is fixed by its interior alone and encodes its entire tidal response. We derive bounds on Love numbers and quasinormal modes from constraints on the impedance that follow from either of two properties of a stable body: it is lossless, meaning that it dissipates no energy, or it is passive, meaning that it never gives back more energy than it has received. For a lossless body, every dynamical Love number is bounded from above and below, exactly in general relativity, in any renormalization scheme and at any scale. Earlier bounds are exact for Newtonian stars but fail for compact stars, because they take the response to arbitrarily fast tides to be zero or non-negative, whereas in general relativity it is negative. The quasinormal modes of a lossless body are also excluded from a region of the complex frequency plane set by the mass and radius. Each known Love number enlarges the excluded region. Conversely, the quasinormal modes bound each Love number from below, given the lower ones. For a passive body of horizon scale, such as a black hole, the static Love number lies in a window that closes onto the vanishing black-hole value as the body&#x27;s surface approaches the horizon.</p>

<p><strong>相关性：</strong>匹配关注项：gr-qc 相关方向、天文/高能相关。</p>

<p><strong>动作：</strong>相关性中等，适合保留链接后续筛选。</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We propose a broadband search for ultralight axion dark matter through the rotation of laser polarization induced by the axion--photon coupling. Two stratospheric balloons flying at altitudes of 30--40 km form a free-space optical link. One balloon sends a linearly polarized beam to the other, which reflects it back to a polarimeter on the first balloon. The long physical baseline provides an alternative to the repeated reflections used in resonant optical cavities. We target axion masses in the range $4\times10^{-15}\,\mathrm{eV}\lesssim m_a\lesssim10^{-11}\,\mathrm{eV}$. The lower edge corresponds to a frequency of about $1\,\mathrm{Hz}$, below which gondola roll about the line of sight can mimic a polarization signal. At $m_a=10^{-14}\,\mathrm{eV}$, a low-cost Pathfinder using flight-demonstrated technology could probe couplings as small as $g_{aγ}\simeq2.8\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$, about a factor of two below the CAST bound. At the same mass, the Feasible today configuration could probe couplings just below the astrophysical bounds considered here, while the Futuristic configuration could reach $g_{aγ}\simeq6.4\times10^{-15}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$, roughly two orders of magnitude below those bounds.</p>
</details>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We investigate how Lorentzian non-commutative smearing affects entanglement and horizon brightened acceleration radiation (HBAR) outside a black hole. For a massless scalar field, tracing over the interior partner gives the exterior quantum channel, its logarithmic negativity, mutual information, and Bell entanglement fidelity. At fixed mass and Killing frequency, increasing the smearing lowers the surface gravity, increases the accessible correlations, and suppresses the leading atomic emission probability. The horizon displacement begins at order $\sqrtΘ$, while the thermal correction at infinity begins at order $Θ$. At fixed local frequency, the redshift introduces a term of order $\sqrtΘ$ at fixed areal radius. Holding the proper distance fixed, it cancels this term and recovers the common Rindler response close to the horizon. For a freely falling atom, emission and absorption obey detailed balance at the Hawking temperature in the leading horizon approximation. Random atomic injection drives the selected mode from the vacuum to a thermal state with an exact relaxation law and nonnegative entropy production. Near equilibrium, we obtain $\dot S_{\rm HBAR}\simeq\dot A_p/4$ when the radiated energy is drawn from horizon heat, where $\dot A_p$ is the positive radiative area loss. When the radiation is assigned to ADM mass loss at fixed smearing length $\ell=4\sqrt{Θ/π}$, the result is $\dot S_{\rm HBAR}\simeq\dot A_p/[4(1-\ell/r_+)]$. The mass dependence of the exterior source thus enters the entropy per unit area lost at order $\sqrtΘ$.</p>

<p>We propose an extension of the integral quantization, applied previously to the semiclassical description of a test particle in the flat Minkowski spacetime, to the Schwarzschild spacetime. This extension yields a local integral quantization. We calculate the transition amplitudes between coherent states of a particle in a mass layer, which effectively leads to a Klein-Gordon-type dynamics. The local integral quantization presented here seems to be applicable to semiclassical quantization of geodesic motion in other curved spacetimes.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We establish sharp decay and regularity thresholds for Minkowski stability in the spherically symmetric Einstein--scalar field system. For small smooth characteristic data measured in weighted derivative seminorms, we prove global stability at borderline decay and half-derivative regularity of the initial data. The resulting developments remain uniformly close to Minkowski spacetime and are future causally geodesically complete. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We investigate the geometry and physical properties of the vacuum type-D accelerating Taub--NUT spacetime in the sector with vanishing Kerr rotation. We recover its normalized metric from the Plebański--Demiański family and a dressed finite binary, tracking the horizon Killing directions through the large-companion limit. The black-hole and acceleration horizons have distinct projective Killing generators, and their intrinsic horizon geometry yields a relation between polar Gaussian curvatures and cone angles that differs from that of the type-I accelerating Taub--NUT solution without requiring a matching of acceleration parameters. We establish separability of the type-D null Hamilton--Jacobi equation and construct an unwrapped radial conformal atlas with four simple-horizon extensions and five angular conformal diagrams. We also examine axial identifications and chronology-violating regions. The resulting geometrical and dynamical distinctions clarify how the two accelerating NUT families differ despite sharing their nonaccelerating and zero-NUT limits; the radial atlas is not claimed to be a unique maximal four-dimensional extension.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Investigating black hole physics in semi-classical gravitational theory is an interesting and important direction. When the backreaction of the conformal anomaly-induced effective stress-energy tensor is taken into account, the black hole spacetime can be largely altered which changes many properties of the black hole. In this work, rather than restricting our attention to the type-A anomaly, we incorporate the type-B anomaly and investigate how it modifies the spacetime structure obtained when only the type-A anomaly is considered. We find that for a specific relation between the central charges and the cosmological constant, the semiclassical Einstein equations can be solved analytically, yielding a metric that takes the exact form of the Kiselev black hole. After deriving the solution, we further discuss the properties of this new black hole solutions. Our work serves as an essential stepping stone for studying black hole geometries under the full conformal anomaly.</p>
