<p><strong>信号：</strong>quasinormal, AdS, de Sitter</p>

<details>
<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We obtain an explicit separation of the coupled gravitational and electromagnetic radiative equations on nonextremal Kerr--Newman--(anti-)de Sitter black holes with nonzero charge and rotation. The construction uses a matrix with constant determinant, chosen independently of the frequency and azimuthal number, and treats the positive- and negative-spin sectors within the same formulation. We establish that this transformation is smooth and invertible throughout the open AdS exterior and between the event and cosmological horizons in dS. The resulting radial and angular systems are valid at arbitrary complex frequencies. We also express regularity of the radiative Newman--Penrose variables at the rotation axes and future horizons as conditions on the separated solutions. In the asymptotically flat limit, the transformation is related to Hintz&#x27;s matrix integrating factor by the allowed frame freedom. These results provide a basis for studying quasinormal modes and black-hole stability in Kerr--Newman--(anti-)de Sitter spacetimes, as well as exploring holographic applications to strongly coupled systems.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) enhances the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) by querying external structured knowledge. However, it can also introduce privacy risks by leaking sensitive information from the retrieval datastore. We propose a simple method to preserve datastore privacy in RAG systems via random projection. By applying the same projection to both datastore embeddings and query embeddings, our method provably preserves semantic similarity between queries and retrieved items while substantially mitigating data extraction attacks. Across multiple RAG architectures and datasets, we show that this lightweight approach achieves superior retrieval and generation performance compared to prior methods which leverage formal differential privacy (DP) guarantees, while exhibiting comparable empirical privacy under strong attack models. Our results for the first time suggest that random projection can serve as a competitive and practical baseline for privacy-preserving RAG systems.</p>
