<details>
<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Gauge freedom in numerical relativity complicates the interpretation of simulation coordinates and the comparison of different descriptions of the same spacetime. We present a data-driven neural network method for learning coordinate maps from numerical metric data to a prescribed target gauge. The network is trained by matching the transformed source metric to the target metric, without assuming a functional form for the coordinate transformation. We first validate the method on analytical Schwarzschild and Kerr geometries, recovering known transformations up to the isometries of the target spacetime. We then apply it to numerical black hole evolutions in moving-puncture coordinates. For a non-spinning black hole, the learned map relates the time-dependent numerical metric to a stationary Schwarzschild metric in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates. For a spinning puncture, the method provides an approximate identification with a Kerr background despite departures from Kerr in the numerical spacetime. We use this identification to construct curvature perturbations and evolve them with the Teukolsky equation, obtaining waveforms that closely match those extracted from the full nonlinear simulation. These results demonstrate that learned coordinate maps can relate numerical spacetimes to analytical backgrounds, with potential applications to cross-code validation and black hole perturbation theory.</p>

<p>In Inflation without an Inflaton (IWI), second-order de Sitter (dS) gravitons generate primordial curvature as the Coulomb-type potential of their gradient stress. Each pair contributes $(p/k)^2$, yielding exponential enhancement over the contributing e-folds. With exact Bunch-Davies evolution, this solution satisfies all four scalar second-order Einstein equations in pure dS. At interferometer frequencies, $Ω_{\rm GW}h^2\simeq2\times10^{-21}$--$3\times10^{-17}$, accessible to BBO for $r\gtrsim0.01$ and ultimate DECIGO for $r\gtrsim10^{-5}$; CMB B-mode searches probe $r\gtrsim10^{-3}$. <p>We study the quasinormal modes (QNMs) of a minimally coupled massless scalar field on the exact spinning Morris-Thorne wormhole. The problem is posed on the complete two-ended geometry, whose asymptotic rest frames rotate relative to each other. After extracting the axis behaviour and the outgoing phases at both infinities, we compactify the radial coordinate and construct a quadratic eigenvalue problem by two-dimensional Chebyshev collocation. The analytic background yields exact constraints on the spectrum. For the sampled sectors $m=1,2,3$ and spins up to $j=19$, every converged frequency is damped and lies outside this window. Independent integration verifies the low-lying modes, while Kerr spectra provide a separate benchmark for the two-dimensional solver. A limiting radial equation explains why the computed mode frequencies approach the upper edge of the superradiant window with frequency offsets proportional to $(mj)^{-1/2}$ at large $mj$. A complete stability assessment requires further analysis of the unresolved scalar spectrum and of perturbations of the coupled metric-matter system.</p>

<p>The XRISM X-ray Observatory has revealed evidence for a &quot;double-cascade&quot; scenario in the Perseus cluster, in which active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback and mergers drive turbulence over distinct radial ranges and spatial scales.
