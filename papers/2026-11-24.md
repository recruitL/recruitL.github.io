<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We investigate quantum-space corrections to the gravitational quasinormal modes (QNMs) of Kerr black holes within the framework of noncommutative geometry. The analysis employs a pseudo-semi-Killing Drinfeld twist, which preserves the classical Kerr geometry as an exact background solution while introducing nontrivial quantum corrections to linearized gravitational perturbations. Working to first order in the black hole spin and the quantum deformation parameter, including their mixed contribution, we derive a Schrödinger-like master equation for axial gravitational perturbations with a frequency- and spin-dependent effective potential. We compute the fundamental QNM frequencies using the WKB approximation and Leaver&#x27;s continued-fraction method.Our results reveal corrections to the QNM oscillation frequencies and damping rates arising from rotation, pure quantum effects, and their interplay.To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic analysis of gravitational metric perturbations in a noncommutative Kerr black hole spacetime using a Drinfeld twist, extending prior work beyond the Schwarzschild case. Our findings demonstrate that quantum deformation effects alter both the oscillation frequencies and damping rates of quasinormal modes, with spin-quantum coupling introducing additional spin-dependent corrections to the Kerr QNM spectrum. Using a Kerr-anchored phenomenological prescription, we estimate the fractional frequency and damping-time shifts for selected merger remnants from the GWTC-3 and GWTC-5.0 catalogs. These estimates illustrate the potential signatures of quantum corrections in gravitational-wave ringdown.</p>

<p>We present analytical expressions for the energy and angular momentum fluxes radiated to infinity by a (nonspinning) point mass in an eccentric-equatorial orbit about a Kerr black hole. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>A classical black hole horizon absorbs completely, and any deviation would be hidden in the bulk. For a scalar field in a rotating Bañados-Teitelboim-Zanelli exterior, we derive an exact boundary response of a corotating passive wall near the horizon. An Airy filter gives its spectrum, and positive energy excludes growing modes. On any frequency band the response identifies a constant wall impedance and the wall position, which enter orthogonally with weights determined by the spectral function. The boundary thus sees a reflecting wall, whereas a perfect absorber is exponentially hidden.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We establish a geometric framework for classifying higher-derivative Ramond-Ramond (RR) couplings on Dp-branes, focusing on the complete scattering amplitude of a closed-string RR state, a gauge field, and two transverse scalars. The low-energy expansion reveals infinite massless poles and local interaction terms that are beyond standard Myers and Taylor expansions. We propose a higher-class deformation of the pullback using mixed Chern-Simons transgression operators to address these complexities. We also propose a generalized class Chern character and three conjectures: (i) the finite dimensionality of independent couplings at fixed derivative order, (ii) the representation of all-order mixed RR-gauge-scalar interactions through finitely many effective even forms, and (iii) the generation of higher-derivative contact terms from finitely many primitive tensor structures. Our framework provides a systematic mathematical foundation for reconstructing D-brane effective actions and exploring their underlying geometric and cohomological structures. We also introduce a higher-class pullback operator \( P_{\infty} \) and mixed transgression operators \( \mathcal{T}_{\infty} \), emphasizing amplitude-derived pole residues and local contact terms as key physical data. This formalism distinguishes admissible tensor structures, presenting characteristic-form aspects as conjectures alongside their validity and their constraints.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We study the effects of bulk and shear viscosity on the damping of axisymmetric oscillations of uniformly rotating relativistic stars, using the first-order viscous hydrodynamics formulation of Bemfica, Disconzi, Noronha and Kovtun (BDNK). The equations of motion are implemented in a two-dimensional numerical code under the Cowling approximation, assuming the fluid evolves on the fixed spacetime of a rotating star. We build two sequences of polytropic stellar models and investigate how damping depends on rotation rate (from the non-rotating case to the mass-shedding limit) and compactness. Our study focuses on the quasi-radial $l=0$ $F$ mode and the $l=1,2,3$ fundamental modes, covering a dataset of 288 perturbed stellar models. For all modes and chosen viscosities, the BDNK runs show that oscillations decay within a few milliseconds, much faster than in the perfect fluid case. The $F$ mode damping is dominated by bulk viscosity, consistent with its global compression of the star, while the $l=2$ and $l=3$ modes are mainly damped by shear viscosity. The $l=1$ mode is largely insensitive to both viscosity coefficients, and its decay rate is dominated by numerical dissipation. Rotation renders the $F$ mode sensitive to shear viscosity. Since the inherent numerical damping does not affect the slopes of the decay rates, our viscous sensitivity estimates faithfully identify the dominant damping mechanism for each mode, shear or bulk viscosity, and reveal robust trends with rotation rate and compactness.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Magnetars display timing anomalies such as glitches and anti-glitches. 1E 2259+586, whose properties in the X-ray band were recently investigated through a joint XMM/IXPE observation campaign, has been observed to have multiple glitches and three anti-glitches, the most recent occurring in May 2025. Although the X-ray emission is currently decaying, the flux, which roughly doubled at the onset of the anti-glitch, had not yet returned to its original level at the time of the IXPE observation, and the pulse profile has also shown significant evolution. No significant detection was found in the phase-averaged and energy-integrated spectrum of this observation. However, despite the significantly shorter exposure, polarisation was clearly detected in the phase-resolved spectrum and the degree of polarisation was found to have a higher best-fitting value, though still statistically consistent, than the previous measurement from 2023, reaching a level &gt;30% in correspondence to the pulse profile minimum. In spite of some slight differences between the two observations, the polarisation trends remain largely consistent and indicate that the source has mostly returned to its pre-event state.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Millisecond pulsars (MSPs) are widely believed to be responsible for the gamma-ray emission of globular clusters (GCs), yet the underlying MSP gamma-ray luminosity function remains uncertain. Existing GC-based determinations of the latter often rely on external prescriptions for the number of MSPs in each cluster. In this work, we constrain the MSP gamma-ray luminosity function in Milky-Way GCs directly from gamma-ray spectral energy distributions (SEDs), establishing an inference strategy that remains computationally viable for joint analyses of large GC samples. We construct a forward model in which each GC hosts a population of MSPs whose luminosities are drawn from a universal log-normal luminosity function. Synthetic GC SEDs are generated by summing individual MSP spectra, and we infer posterior distributions using first a likelihood-based nested sampling method and then a simulation-based inference (SBI) approach. We show how the likelihood-based method becomes rapidly impractical as the number of jointly modelled clusters (and cluster-specific latent parameters) increases, whereas the SBI approach scales mildly with sample size and reproduces the likelihood-based posteriors in regimes where direct sampling is feasible. We then apply the SBI pipeline to Fermi-LAT SEDs from the 4FGL-DR4 catalog for a sample of 36 GC-associated sources, augmented by dedicated phase-resolved ON/OFF SEDs for two clusters hosting individually detected MSPs. For the real data sample, we obtain informative constraints on the luminosity-function parameters consistent with previous analyses that constrain the cluster-specific MSP counts via complementary multi-wavelength information.</p>
