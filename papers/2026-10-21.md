<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Space-borne gravitational wave (GW) detectors such as TianQin are expected to detect GW standard sirens for cosmological parameter estimation. All previous studies of TianQin adopted the $Λ$CDM model to generate mock standard siren data. In this work, we quantitatively investigate how the choice of background cosmological model affects mock TianQin standard siren (TQSS) catalogs and the corresponding cosmological implications. Adopting the $Λ$CDM model and a redshift-binned dark energy (DE) reconstruction model (the wbin model), we consider the bright standard sirens from massive black hole binary mergers and generate two mock TQSS catalogs, i.e. TQSS1 and TQSS2, respectively. We investigate the difference of the catalogs and further explore their cosmological implications within the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) model, both individually and in combination with DESI DR2 baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data, $\textit{Planck}$ 2018 cosmic microwave background (CMB) data, and DESY5 type Ia supernova (SN Ia) data. We find that there are only small differences between TQSS1 and TQSS2 catalogs, with a maximum relative difference of $1.48\%$ in the distance-redshift relation. In addition, either the TQSS1 or TQSS2 catalog alone gives cosmological constraints similar to those obtained from SN data, implying that these two catalogs can serve as a complement to mainstream cosmological observations. Most importantly, we find that both TQSS1+DESI DR2+CMB+DESY5 and TQSS2+DESI DR2+CMB+DESY5 are still expected to favor dynamical DE, with these two combinations showing a preference for dynamical DE at $2.3σ$ and $3.8σ$, respectively. Adopting different massive black hole seed models does not impact the cosmological implications of the mock TQSS catalogs. Our study indicates the importance of considering different cosmological models in the standard siren forecasts for DE.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We determine the loss of mechanical angular momentum $L^μ_{\rm loss}$ of two gravitationally scattering bodies at $\mathcal{O}(G^4)$ using worldline quantum field theory (WQFT). The gravitational memory effect, a phenomenon of the early- and late-time behaviour of the gravitational field, leads to static contributions to $L^μ_{\rm loss}$. We illuminate how these arise: the linear memory contributes through so-called static correlators, while the nonlinear memory vanishes at this order due to symmetry considerations. The computation of the remaining, dynamical part resembles that of the $\mathcal{O}(G^4)$ impulse. We generate its integrand using a reformulation of the WQFT Feynman rules that conserves four-momentum at every vertex and plainly exhibits the classical loops. The three-loop integrals are reduced to master integrals, whose differential equations and boundary matching in the post-Newtonian limit we describe in some detail. In contrast to conservative effects at this order, only polylogarithms appear. While individual contributions to the dynamical part are divergent, these cancel in $L^μ_{\rm loss}$. The quadratic-in-radiation part of our result reproduces earlier results, while the complete result agrees with existing post-Newtonian results.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Quantum-gravitational spacetime foam varying in space and in time imprints, respectively, a transverse phase decoherence and an achromatic, stochastic arrival-time jitter on light from distant sources. We show that the jitter modifies the first-order temporal coherence $g^{(1)}(τ)$ and hence the observed spectrum, providing a spectroscopic probe of the temporal variation, complementary to existing imaging-channel constraints on the spatial variation. In the dynamical-foam limit, where the foam decorrelates within the light&#x27;s coherence time, the spectrum splits into a coherent line core suppressed by $e^{-σ_φ^2}$, with $σ_φ^2$ the variance of the foam-induced phase jitter, and an incoherent pedestal, yielding a spectral shape-distortion bound on the foam exponent $α$. The pedestal-independent line-width and CMB bounds are floors below the holographic value $α= 2/3$, consistent with and excluding no part of the imaging window reported for GRB\,221009A; only the conditional TeV survival bound ($α\gtrsim0.74$), which holds only for near-Planck-refresh foam ($τ_{\rm foam}\lesssim4\times10^{-28}$\,s), would exclude it --- and, with it, the window.</p>
