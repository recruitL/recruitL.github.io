<p><strong>摘要判断：</strong>The quasinormal modes (QNMs) of a black hole are defined by keeping only ingoing waves at the horizon.</p>
<p><strong>相关性：</strong>匹配关注项：致密双星建模、QNM/黑洞扰动、gr-qc 相关方向。 这是 gr-qc 每日新增或更新条目。</p>

<p><strong>动作：</strong>优先精读，并检查方法、近似和可复现材料。</p>
<details>
<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>The quasinormal modes (QNMs) of a black hole are defined by keeping only ingoing waves at the horizon. We ask what horizon regularity requires of the outgoing waves this condition discards. For a scalar-like perturbation, bounded first derivatives in a frame regular at the future horizon rule out outgoing waves that decay more slowly than the surface gravity $κ$, and smoothness singles out a ladder of frequencies, $ω=mΩ_{\rm H}-ijκ$ with $j=1,2,\dots$ and $Ω_{\rm H}$ the horizon angular velocity. We call these the horizon modes. For Schwarzschild black holes and the dominant $\ell=m=2$ Kerr modes, only the fundamental QNM decays more slowly than $κ$. These local statements leave the QNM spectrum unchanged but matter for merger ringdowns, because a source falling through the horizon drives the exterior at the horizon frequencies. Each QNM then has a driven and a free part, and the free part takes over only once the driving decays faster than the mode. The fundamental mode separates from the driving early. The overtones stay driven until the source is hidden behind the potential barrier, so overtone amplitudes fitted near the signal peak are in general not those of free oscillations. In GW250114, a ringdown model of the fundamental mode plus the first horizon mode, $ω=2Ω_{\rm H}-iκ$, is preferred over the fundamental mode plus its first overtone, with Bayes factors between 2 and 30, for start times from $2M_f$ before to $8M_f$ after coalescence. The horizon-mode fit returns a remnant that is heavier and spins faster than the inspiral predicts, as expected from nonlinear backreaction: the horizon that emits the mode has not yet settled to its final mass and spin. Zero-noise injections of numerical-relativity-calibrated waveforms reproduce both the preference and the shift, which are therefore features of general relativity.</p>
</details>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>The Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) will observe massive black hole binaries (MBHBs) for days to months before merger, offering opportunities for advance electromagnetic follow-up. To enable such observations, inference methods must provide rapid, continuously updated, and statistically calibrated posterior estimates, while remaining robust to overlapping sources. We present TRUFFLE (Time-evolving Rapid Updates For Follow-up of LISA Events), a simulation-based inference method for MBHBs in LISA data designed to meet these requirements. Our approach uses two networks, selected according to the time to coalescence relative to the end of the analysis window, $Δt_c$. An early-warning network, operating at $Δt_c \in [4~\mathrm{h}, 2~\mathrm{d}]$, estimates the chirp mass, mass ratio, and coalescence time to support electromagnetic follow-up scheduling. A near-merger network, operating at $Δt_c \in [-1~\mathrm{h}, +4~\mathrm{h}]$, infers nine parameters, including the sky position needed for telescope pointing. Both networks process the most recent six days of data using multiband time- and frequency-domain representations, which are summarized by a transformer to condition a normalizing flow. The networks are trained on the full orbit-driven LISA response and amortized over a 30-day range of observation epochs. The near-merger posterior can be corrected through likelihood-based importance sampling, with neural inference taking seconds and likelihood reweighting approximately five minutes on a single GPU. We obtain unbiased posteriors in the presence of overlapping sources, including plunging extreme-mass-ratio inspirals, resolvable Galactic binaries, and additional MBHBs merging up to two hours before the primary merger.</p>

<p>We develop numerical relativity evolution schemes based on Plebanski&#x27;s chiral formulation of general relativity and its 3+1 form, Ashtekar&#x27;s variables. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Neutron stars provide a natural laboratory for superfluid matter under extreme conditions. Its nonlinear dynamics is central to pulsar glitches, their possible links to fast radio bursts, and tidal interactions in inspiralling binaries. <p>We study the production of gravitational wave (GW) backgrounds from slowly-evolving sources in the early Universe, following the Constant-in-Time (CiT) model, presented and validated against simulations of decaying turbulence in arXiv:2201.05630. <p>We reconstruct the magnetic nonlinear electrodynamics (NED) source of the Dymnikova geometry on the branch \(\Iinv&gt;0\) and analyze its electromagnetic perturbations on the fixed background. The extraordinary characteristic cone gives a transverse speed \(v_\perp^2=(3u-5)/2\), with \(u=r^3/P^3\), which becomes superluminal for \(r&gt;r_{\rm lum}=(7/3)^{1/3}P\).
