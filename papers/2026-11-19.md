<p><strong>相关性：</strong>匹配关注项：致密双星建模、LVK/LISA/引力波观测、QNM/黑洞扰动。 这是 gr-qc 每日新增或更新条目。</p>

<details>
<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Black-hole ringdown is usually modeled as a linear superposition of quasinormal modes, and fits to gravitational-wave observations increasingly include high overtones. However, the overtone sum does not necessarily converge: overtones are more strongly damped and thus decay faster in time, but their amplitudes in the Green function (the signal at an observer produced by an impulse at a source point) grow, so the mode description may break down before a finite time. We show that for a rotating (Kerr) black hole this time has a clear geometric meaning. For highly damped modes, the amplitudes grow exponentially with the optical distance from the source and the observer to a characteristic &quot;bounce&quot; radius close to the black hole, defined as the location where the mode amplitude is smallest. For one family of modes, the amplitudes are also suppressed the farther the source and observer sit from the equatorial plane. Rotation therefore makes the onset of convergence depend on direction as well as distance, extending the geometric picture from bounce radii to bounce surfaces. Combining these effects gives a sufficient condition for absolute convergence. For spin $a/M = 0.7$, we map how this time varies with source and observer position, and amplitudes computed numerically reproduce the predicted spatial dependence to better than one percent. These results set geometric limits on when the quasinormal-mode sums converge.</p>
</details>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We present \texttt{StochAXtic}, a package for computing the dimensionless energy density spectrum $Ω_{\mathrm{GW}}(f)$ of the stochastic gravitational-wave background generated by compact binary coalescences. Rather than sampling binary populations via Monte Carlo, \texttt{StochAXtic} exploits the \emph{scale invariance} of the waveforms: at fixed mass ratio, spins and tidal deformabilities, the waveform depends on the total mass only through the dimensionless combination $Mf$. This allows all computationally expensive waveform evaluations using \texttt{LALSuite} to be performed once and stored in a population-independent lookup table. The subsequent integrals over mass and redshift can then be recast as convolutions and evaluated efficiently using fast Fourier transforms. We further employ truncated singular value decomposition to compress the lookup table, reducing both computational cost and memory requirements. Once the table has been computed and cached, \texttt{StochAXtic} evaluates $Ω_{\mathrm{GW}}(f)$ in a few milliseconds, achieving a relative numerical error below $10^{-3}$ at frequencies up to $1$ kHz. Its results agree with those of \texttt{popstock} within the latter&#x27;s statistical uncertainties. Implemented entirely in \texttt{JAX}, \texttt{StochAXtic} also provides gradients of $Ω_{\mathrm{GW}}(f)$ with respect to population hyperparameters, enabling its direct integration with gradient-based inference methods including Hamiltonian Monte Carlo.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Gravitational waves (GWs) propagate through the universe from sources at high redshift, experiencing the gravitational fields of the intervening matter distribution. Halos composed of ultralight dark matter (ULDM) contain strong density fluctuations, reflecting the interference pattern in the field distribution of the ultralight bosons. In the wave-optics regime, ULDM fluctuations act as a diffractive grating, imprinting frequency-dependent modulations of GWs, in addition to magnification and other gravitational lensing effects. <p>The formation and evolution of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) remain major open questions in astrophysics. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Gravitational wave astronomy has opened a new window onto black holes and the dynamics of strong-field gravity, while accretion flows offer a complementary and natural laboratory for probing strong-field physics. In this work, we show that the gravitational wave emission from an initially tilted accretion disk carries a distinct imprint of its orientation. We perform three dimensional general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations and evaluate the gravitational waves through Teukolsky equation. For an aligned disk, the emission is concentrated in low-$|m|$ modes, where $m$ is the azimuthal quantum number. For the initially tilted disk, higher-$|m|$ modes make a larger relative contribution. As the tilted flow subsequently aligns with the black hole spin, the contribution of $|m|\ge2$ modes decreases, showing strong correlation with the tilt angle. This indicates that the gravitational waves generated by the black hole environment carries geometric information about the surrounding matter. Our results establish a route towards using multimode gravitational radiation to probe the geometry and dynamical evolution of accretion flows in the strong-field region.</p>

<p>Combining the data of multiple pulsar timing array (PTA) collaborations increases the sensitivity to nanohertz gravitational waves (GWs) and other signals correlated between pulsars. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>The retarded Green&#x27;s function of gravitational waves in a spatially flat Friedmann--Lemaître--Robertson--Walker universe contains, besides the light-cone term, a tail supported inside the light cone. We formulate the tail, for homogeneous and isotropic backgrounds, as the solution of a ``\textit{Goursat problem},&#x27;&#x27; {\emph{i.e.}}, a hyperbolic partial differential equation with boundary conditions given on two intersecting characteristic curves. In this sense, we fill the interior of the light cone. Its first correction, the effect of the tail, depends on the curvature potential integrated along the light cone. The applications of the tail, the power spectrum of a background of bursts, and the memory waveform of a single burst, both of which require convolving the Green&#x27;s function with its source, are presented in follow-up papers.</p>
