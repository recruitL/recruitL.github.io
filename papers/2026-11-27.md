<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Gravitational-wave measurements of the effective spin $χ_\text{eff}$ and mass ratio $q$ help distinguish binary black hole formation channels, motivating a search for structure in the $χ_\text{eff} - q$ plane. However, both these quantities are known to be strongly correlated in GW parameter estimation since both enter the leading-order spin-orbit term together, and thus, the detectors cannot easily tell them apart. In this work, we harness the susceptibility of such measurements to waveform systematics by using injection-recovery studies with NRSur7dq4 as the true signal and SEOBNRv5PHM and IMRPhenomTPHM for parameter inferences. Our study shows that, though there can be some parameter space in the $χ_\text{eff} - q$ plane, primarily in the unequal-mass regime, there can be some impacts from waveform systematics; overall, at a population level, the waveform systematics for the current detectors at their design sensitivity are not a bottleneck in recovering the underlying true population properties. We find that astrophysical properties such as $χ_\text{eff} - q$ correlations, which are signatures of different formation channels, are unlikely to be spuriously induced by waveform systematics, and inferences about formation channels based on such correlations are likely robust to waveform systematics at present, though some events that are in the parameter space of large impact can show some outlier behaviour. We find that some of the GW events such as GW190403$\_$051519 and GW240527$\_$230910 are in the vicinity of the $χ_\text{eff} - q$ parameter space, where large waveform systematics can arise, and these events are more susceptible to waveform systematics than others in GWTC-5. This study will play a vital role in precision astrophysical population inference from GW data in the future and in assessing the possibility of waveform systematics.</p>

<p>We present phenjax, a JAX implementation of the precessing time-domain IMRPhenomT family of models for the gravitational wave signal emitted in compact binary coalescence. <p><strong>信号：</strong>quasi-normal, QNM, QNMs, AdS, singularity</p>

<details>
<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We show that the holographic spectral function of a neutral massless scalar on the extremal Reissner-Nordstrom black brane in $AdS_5$ admits an elegant formula in terms of the quasi-normal modes (QNMs) and branch-cut data. In the extremal case, the spectral data comprises the zeros of the boundary source function, i.e. the QNMs, as well as its phase discontinuity across the branch cut on the negative imaginary axis of the complex frequency plane. This formula neatly factorizes into an infinite product over the QNM poles and a factor encoding the branch cut discontinuity. We derive this pole-cut formula as the zero-temperature limit of the QNM product formula for holographic thermal correlators, with the branch-cut contribution arising from the coalescence of the QNMs on the negative imaginary axis. We also clarify the role of the near-horizon $AdS_2$ region in this context. Consistency with the OPE of the boundary correlator provides extremal sum-rules involving both the QNMs as well as the phase discontinuity function. We also find that this holographic consistency forces the asymptotic form of the QNMs to be related to the asymptotics of the phase discontinuity function. We verify our formula and these OPE constraints numerically. We also demonstrate that, in the time domain, the pole and cut factors each produce complex-time singularities, whose location is determined by the optical distance between the boundary and the black-brane singularity. We expect the pole-cut factorization structure of spectral functions to hold for more general fluctuations of extremal black branes.</p>

<p>We propose a background independent quantization of vacuum general relativity on four dimensional Lorentzian spacetimes. Starting from first order gravity, we solve the gravitational constraints on intersecting null cones of a bulk subregion and obtain the reduced phase space at finite $G_N$. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly applied to time series reasoning. However, current evaluations often give models events in chronological order and already matched to the time series. This makes it unclear whether models use timestamps to connect events with observations or simply take shortcuts. To expose this ambiguity, we reformulate time series reasoning from a world-to-series projection perspective, viewing timestamps as explicit indices that connect numerical observations and external events to shared world states. We identify three structures that timestamps can make explicit: temporal causal structure, time semantics, and event-series alignment. Building on this perspective, we develop a diagnostic protocol that distinguishes timestamp-grounded reasoning from correlational shortcuts, a distinction that accuracy-based benchmarks cannot make. Controlled interventions on 310 questions from two tasks show that high accuracy can persist when timestamps are replaced by relative offsets or removed, while breaking event-series alignment induces systematic failures. When context is pre-aligned, models barely need timestamps. When order is missing, they use dates to recall world knowledge. Without dates, they rely on correlational shortcuts and rarely notice incorrect timestamps. These findings show that high accuracy on pre-aligned, ordered inputs does not establish that a model uses timestamps to synchronize observations with the world. We release our code, model outputs, and trajectory labels at https://github.com/tingrui-huang/timestamps-matter.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Proverbs, as repositories of traditional wisdom, have informed human reasoning for millennia. Yet the collective prescriptions they encode have remained largely unquantified. We introduce a computational framework to analyze nearly 50,000 English-language proverbs. Using large language models, validated against human judgments, we map the topical landscape of proverbs, assess their prescriptive power, and quantify their recommendations on key dimensions of human judgment and behavior. We find that most proverbs are prescriptive, offering guidance in domains under individual control, such as preparation and prudent action, whereas proverbs about uncontrollable circumstances, such as fate and mortality, tend to describe rather than prescribe. When prescriptive proverbs take a consistent stance, they favor caution over risk, long-term over short-term outcomes, honesty over deception, and kindness over malice, and show a moderate tendency toward suspicion over trust. On dimensions involving genuine trade-offs, especially autonomy versus compliance, proverbs provide contradicting advice rather than a single recommendation. This analysis provides a quantitative map of the collective prescriptions in a culture&#x27;s proverbial wisdom, revealing a core of prudent, honest, and forward-looking guidance alongside varied advice where circumstances dictate the best course of action.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Knowledge distillation enables the deployment of large language models through smaller student models, but the compression process can introduce regressions in trustworthiness even when downstream performance is preserved. Repairing such failures is challenging because the original distillation corpus is often unavailable, while direct preference optimization may alter capabilities inherited from the teacher. We propose a targeted repair framework based on the proximal Bregman response function (PBRF) that addresses both limitations. We estimate the curvature required for PBRF using a surrogate proxy dataset, removing dependence on the original transfer corpus, and extend the framework to preference-based supervision through an odds-ratio objective. This enables the use of preferred and dispreferred response pairs while constraining changes to the distilled model. Our results show that the proposed approach improves targeted trustworthiness behaviors for both pre-training and post-training models while largely preserving downstream performance, providing a practical alternative to re-distillation or unconstrained preference optimization. Our experiment results show that across different distilled models, the trustworthy regression can be improved by up to 8% with less than 1% decrement in perplexity.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Many languages of instruction in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) are poorly represented in the data used to train language models. Before these models can support early-grade literacy in such languages, we need to know whether they hold the linguistic knowledge of each language that literacy teaching relies on. To test this, we present the Linguistic Knowledge Benchmark and the reproducible, largely automated pipeline that turns curriculum documents, teacher guides and pupil books into a vetted question set organised on a fixed grid of nine linguistic domains and seven grade levels. The models most practical for low-resource classrooms are therefore the least capable in the languages they would serve. We release a 20-item sample per language, all pipeline prompts, open-source code, and a public leaderboard. This is the first rung of a three-level evaluation ladder for measuring and generating decodable text to support foundational literacy.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Looped Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures revisit layer parameters across depths. This design increases effective depth without adding parameters. Recent looped MoE studies report substantial gains over the standard MoE at matched budgets (parameters, FLOPs and KV cache). However, the reported gains still confound parameter revisit with other design choices, e.g., network shape and residual scale. We ask, what does parameter revisit itself buy? Using matched non-revisit controls, we find that parameter revisit itself buys almost nothing. We argue that revisit only offers a freedom an MoE already has, i.e., trading experts for depth (more layers, fewer experts each) at fixed parameters. First, at matched budgets, we decompose the state-of-the-art looped recipe, with revisit last. A non-revisit control retains all the gain. Second, beyond matched budgets, we evaluate revisit by adding compute at fixed parameters. One extra visit gains more than doubling the experts. Yet without revisit, a deeper standard MoE at equal parameters and compute obtains 90.2% of that gain under Muon. Overall, gains come from network shape, the optimizer and added compute, which a standard MoE can adopt. Accordingly, we propose LoopTitan-Base as a stronger MoE baseline. Under Muon, it matches the strongest looped recipe without revisit or residual scale. We open-source LoopTitan and release 17 pretrained models (including LoopTitan-Base and the control). Our experiment record is public as GitHub issues, with predictions pre-registered and the full exploration history kept. This is our Phase 1 technical report, laying the foundation for follow-up studies.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>In the Standard Model, paramagnetic electric dipole moments (EDMs) are dominated by kaon exchange, which pairs the CP-violating short-distance transition $s\to d\,e^+e^-$ with a CP-conserving weak kaon-nucleon coupling. Long-distance two-photon exchange, which dominates $K_L\to e^+e^-$, gives an electron vertex about thirty-five times larger than the short-distance one and could obscure this origin. We show that it does not: the two-photon vertex conserves CP and must be paired with the CP-violating penguin component of the nucleon coupling, whose relative size is $3\times10^{-4}$, so that electromagnetic corrections stay at the few-percent level. Paramagnetic EDMs therefore measure $\mathrm{Im}(V_{ts}^*V_{td})$ and, together with the CP-violating $S$ wave of $K_S\toμ^+μ^-$ and $K_L\toπ^0ν\barν$, determine the same imaginary coordinate of a kaon CKM plane with electrons, muons, and neutrinos, without $B$-decay input. Lepton-specific new physics, or CP violation that bypasses kaon exchange, would separate these determinations. Testing their common CKM origin calls for coordinated progress in molecular EDM searches, rare-kaon experiments at LHCb, NA62, and KOTO, and lattice determinations of weak nucleon matrix elements.</p>

<p>J191213.72-441045.1 (J1912-4410) is the second white dwarf pulsar to be discovered. These binary systems consist of a white dwarf primary with a cool companion and undergo pulsed emission from radio to X-ray wavelengths. The prototype of the class, AR Scorpii (AR Sco), exhibits a lack of accretion, which led to suggestions that the spin-down of the white dwarf is powering the system&#x27;s non-thermal emission.
