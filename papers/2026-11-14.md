<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Multiparameter gravitational-wave tests of general relativity (GR) are limited by strong correlations among post-Newtonian (PN) phase deformations and the computational cost of Bayesian inference. We present DINGO-TGR, a neural posterior estimation framework that jointly infers binary-source parameters and six PN phase deformations within the TIGER parametrization, using IMRPhenomXPHM waveforms with spin precession and higher-order modes. Importance sampling reweights the neural proposal samples, while principal component analysis (PCA) identifies two well-constrained combinations of PN deformations. We validate the framework using 16 zero-noise GR injections and 12 beyond-GR injections, recovering GR-consistent posteriors in the former and leading-mode shifts consistent with the injected deformations in the latter. For GW150914 and GW250114, our results agree with independent \texttt{bilby-tgr} analyses using \texttt{dynesty}. After training, inference including importance sampling takes approximately 38 minutes and 4.4 hours, respectively, compared with 177 and 238 hours for conventional sampling. GW150914 exhibits a mild offset from GR in the leading PCA mode, whereas GW250114 is consistent with GR. To assess the significance of the GW150914 offset, we analyze 1000 GW150914-like GR injections with independent Gaussian-noise realizations and fixed detector power spectral densities. The resulting empirical upper-tail $p$-value is $11.5\%$, indicating that the observed offset does not reject the adopted GR null model at the 5\% significance level. Our results demonstrate the potential of importance-sampling-corrected neural inference for efficient multiparameter GR tests and large-scale injection-based significance assessments.</p>

<details>
<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Continuous gravitational waves (CWs) from neutron stars beyond the Galactic Center can be lensed by the supermassive black hole in Sgr A$^*$. Since the signal lasts far longer than the lensing time delay, the two geometric-optics images overlap and interfere. Transverse source motion during the observation makes their magnifications and relative time delay evolve, producing evolving amplitude and phase modulations absent from standard CW templates. Using lensed and unlensed injections in Gaussian noise, we quantify their effect on a Hough-based semicoherent search through a semicoherent mismatch. Recovery is governed by the competition between lensing amplification and waveform modulation. Smaller impact parameters generally improve recovery through stronger magnification, while higher transverse velocities partially offset this gain through faster modulation, especially at small impact parameters and short source-lens distances, where image interference is appreciable. The response also depends on the intrinsic signal strength. Loud signals exhibit small fractional changes, with net losses in some configurations, whereas weaker signals yield broader mismatch distributions extending to more negative values, indicating greater enhancement. A population study, sampling lensing parameters from astrophysically motivated velocity and source-lens distance distributions, reproduces the strong dependence on signal strength. Differences between populations are modest, but high-velocity, short source-lens distance populations tend towards larger mismatches. Lensing effects therefore cannot be accounted for by rescaling search sensitivity with the magnification alone; reinterpreting directed Galactic Center search sensitivities and upper limits under the lensing hypothesis requires injection studies with the full lensed waveform across the lensing parameter space.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>The Galactic chemical evolution (GCE) of potassium (K) and of most other odd-Z elements remains poorly understood: models generally underproduce the observed [K/Fe] over a wide metallicity range, depending on the adopted massive star yields. A proposed site for efficient K nucleosynthesis is the merger between the oxygen and carbon shells (C-O shell merger) that can occur in massive stars a few hours before core collapse. Some GCE models with specific yield sets have been shown to reconcile predictions with observations. Here we re-analyse these yield sets to quantify the contribution of C-O shell mergers to K production and what the Galactic evolution of K implies about the occurrence of these events. We analyse 194 pre-supernova models from three independent yield sets, covering a wide range of initial masses, metallicities and rotational velocities, and including both single and binary-stripped stars. We identify the progenitors undergoing a C-O shell merger and compute GCE models with and without their contribution. In all three sets, the enhanced K production can be traced back to a small number of C-O merger progenitors, which eject up to ~10-50 times more K than non-merger progenitors of similar mass. Without them, no model reproduces the observations and [K/Fe] is sub-solar over most of the metallicity range. With them, [K/Fe] is enhanced only within the metallicity window where mergers occur in each grid. Binarity itself plays a minor role, as single stars alone produce similar amounts of K. Since the K synthesised in a C-O merger is essentially primary, the observed nearly linear increase of [K/H] with [Fe/H] can be reproduced only if C-O shell mergers occur with a similar frequency at all metallicities. K could therefore be used to infer the occurrence rate of C-O shell mergers in massive stars as a function of time.</p>
