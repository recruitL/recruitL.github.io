<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>It is commonly assumed that the mass of dark matter particles cannot be determined from their gravitational effects when dark matter behaves as collisionless matter. Here we show that extreme-mass-ratio inspirals observable by millihertz gravitational-wave observatories such as the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) could measure the mass of ultralight dark matter particles in sufficiently dense dark matter spikes. The separation of scales between the two black holes allows ultralight dark matter to form a collisionless density spike around the massive primary while retaining wave effects in its interaction with the stellar-mass companion. We incorporate wave-dependent accretion and dynamical friction into relativistic inspiral waveforms and jointly infer the dark matter particle mass, spike normalisation and binary parameters from simulated LISA signals. For massive black holes at the low-mass end of the LISA population ($M\sim 10^5 M_\odot$), our forecasts show that particle masses spanning $10^{-14}-10^{-11}\,\mathrm{eV}$ could be measured to within an order of magnitude.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>The high-mass black hole population inferred from ground-based observations can substantially affect the number of binaries detectable by the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA). Essentially all of these mergers would be detectable by a reference Cosmic Explorer configuration if it is operating at merger. Ground-based measurements of the high-mass population can therefore inform both LISA forecasts and the prospects for observing the same binaries through inspiral and merger.</p>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We study scalar and electromagnetic perturbations of a Schwarzschild black hole (BH) surrounded by a dark matter halo described by the King density profile. The lapse function can be expressed as $1-2[M+M_{\rm DM}(r)]/r$, where $M_{\rm DM}(r)$ is the halo mass enclosed within radius $r$; thus, the halo affects the geometry through its enclosed-mass function. We compute the quasinormal frequencies using the sixth-order WKB method and its Padé-improved version. Rewriting the lapse function in terms of the inverse hyperbolic sine avoids the severe numerical cancellations that arise when evaluating its high-order derivatives. For the configurations examined, the fundamental frequencies agree with those extracted from time-domain evolution to within $0.2\%$.The halo lowers both the oscillation frequency and the damping rate. The change of the real part is almost entirely accounted for by the halo mass enclosed by the photon sphere, and the additional decrease of the damping rate follows the ratio of the Lyapunov exponent to the angular frequency of the circular null orbit. Since part of the halo lies inside the horizon in this metric, we also place the halo outside the BH by means of an Einstein cluster. The frequencies then decrease by a similar amount, but the real part follows the Schwarzschild value multiplied by the gravitational redshift factor of the halo. A jump in the density at the inner edge of the halo scatters the waves and changes the damping rate by up to about $9\%$. For a continuous density profile, deviations from the redshift relation remain below about $1.5\%$ for $r_{\rm in}=3M$ and below about $0.5\%$ for the cases with $r_{\rm in}\geq4M$. No unstable modes are found within the perturbation sectors and parameter configurations examined.</p>

<p>We investigate whether a stochastic spin-2 gauge-field background can stabilize the chromomagnetic condensate of axion--SU(2) Yang--Mills theory at fixed axion Noether charge in flat spacetime. <details>
<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>When an LLM group&#x27;s discussion is compressed into a short memory for a final decision maker, what should the memory keep? Two natural heuristics keep the rarest claims or the claims relevant to the decision criteria. On hidden-profile tasks both look attractive, because how widely a fact is held is confounded with whether it helps. We introduce CrossProfile, a diagnostic benchmark of 60 templates with automatically verified answer keys, in which the ownership of supporting, decoy, and irrelevant facts is crossed while their text is held fixed (480 tasks). Across Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and two DeepSeek models, both heuristics fail in predictable places. Selecting by relevance readmits decoys, because decoys are on topic: even a perfect relevance filter loses 23 to 31 points to rarest-first selection in the classic hidden-profile configuration. Rarest-first selection is no better than random selection on average and wins only where the rare facts happen to be the useful ones. In both cases accuracy follows the balance of evidence the memory keeps, not the policy that kept it. The decision model adds a third failure, a form of social proof: it gives extra weight to facts attributed to many agents. Removing the attribution tags, or reassigning them at random with line order fixed, raises accuracy in the classic configuration by 13 to 24 points. In natural discussions the evidence is lost before any memory acts: the complete transcript yields lower accuracy than no notes at all.</p>
</details>

<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We experimentally demonstrate a universal, parameter-independent test for asymmetric source discrimination below the diffraction limit. The test exploits spatial-mode demultiplexing (SPADE) and single-photon detection to discriminate the presence of a faint source in the vicinity of a brighter one. We develop a complete theory of the test in the presence of arbitrary modal crosstalk and show that the crosstalk-affected test retains a favorable error exponent over diffraction-limited direct imaging, provided that the crosstalk remains below a threshold C max ≃ 1 / 9 . This establishes a quantitative condition under which the subdiffraction advantage of SPADE survives realistic imperfections. We experimentally test the scheme for a range of source separations and intensity ratios, without requiring prior knowledge of these parameters. The measured false-negative error rates are in good agreement with the theoretical model and systematically outperform direct imaging. These results demonstrate that SPADE provides a robust and experimentally accessible advantage for asymmetric subdiffraction hypothesis testing in the photon-starved regime, even in the presence of realistic modal crosstalk.</p>
