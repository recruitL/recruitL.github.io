<summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>The proposed decihertz gravitational-wave (GW) band $(\sim 10^{-2}-1\,\Hz)$ will observe the late inspiral of intermediate mass-ratio inspirals (IMRIs), binaries with mass ratios $10^2 \lesssim q \lesssim 10^4$ that assemble dynamically in dense stellar environments and are expected to retain measurable eccentricity. Search and parameter-estimation pipelines are predominantly built around circular templates, and we test this assumption. Using black-hole perturbation theory waveforms from \textsc{FastEMRIWaveforms} for a representative IMRI ($M=10^4\,\modot$, $q=100$, observed for its final $10\,\hr$), we compute fitting factors between eccentric signals and circular templates, maximizing over the template&#x27;s total mass and mass ratio with a differential-evolution search, for the B-DECIGO, LGWA-Si, and IndIGO-D noise curves and a flat PSD. The mismatch grows with initial eccentricity $e_0$ and crosses the standard $3\%$ threshold at $e_0\simeq3\times10^{-2}$, well within the range allowed by the waveform model and expected from dynamical formation. The best-fitting circular templates also bias the total mass and mass ratio by several percent at $e_0=0.1$. Combining the mismatch with the signal-to-noise-dependent distinguishability criterion, we find that for a source at $1\,\mathrm{Gpc}$, eccentricities as small as $e_0\simeq5\times10^{-4}$ (B-DECIGO and IndIGO-D) and $5\times10^{-3}$ (LGWA-Si) are resolvable, one to two orders of magnitude below where detection efficiency degrades. The loss of match arises from the excitation of radial Teukolsky harmonics, which spread power into sidebands that circular templates cannot represent, and from the slower orbital decay of eccentric binaries at fixed initial semi-latus rectum. Robust detection and parameter estimation of IMRIs with DECIGO, LGWA, and IndIGO-D will require eccentric waveform models and template banks.</p>

<p>Gravitational-wave lensing in the wave-optics regime is sensitive to the structure of the lens beyond its total mass: to its exterior radial profile and, as we show, to the boundary condition at its centre. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>We develop a fractional-calculus-based framework for evaluating gravitational light deflection in static, spherically symmetric spacetimes. By introducing a suitable transformation of the radial coordinate, the deflection integral is recast into an Abel-type integral equation, from which the associated kernel is reconstructed using fractional integration. The leading singular behaviour near the photon sphere is isolated analytically, and the remaining regular part is approximated by a finite Chebyshev polynomial expansion. This combination retains the correct strong-field behaviour while providing an efficient numerical representation of the deflection integral. We demonstrate the accuracy of the method using the Schwarzschild spacetime as a benchmark and compare the resulting deflection angles with the standard strong-deflection approximation and a two-term weak-deflection expansion. We further apply the method to calculate strong-field lensing observables, including the limiting angular position, angular separation, and relative magnification of relativistic images, for the supermassive black holes Sgr A*. The results show that the proposed approach provides an accurate and flexible means of evaluating gravitational deflection angles across a broad range of closest-approach radii. The framework can be extended to other static, spherically symmetric black-hole geometries, including solutions arising in modified theories of gravity.</p>

<p>We construct quantum reference frames (QRFs) in quantum field theory, using localized modes of a scalar field as frames for its shift symmetry. <summary>英文摘要</summary>
<p>Working within covariant phase space formalism, we establish \textit{gravity-thermodynamics correspondence} for generic dynamical spacetimes. We derive local first and second laws of thermodynamics on general null boundaries, for generic non-stationary solutions to Einstein gravity {with minimally coupled matter} for physical observers that we carefully specify. Entropy variation, which receives contribution only from the gravitational sector, is the surface charge variation associated with a vector field generating the null boundary, and temperature is its non-affinity parameter (divided by $2π$). The statement of the second law in our setting is that the entropy variation evaluated along the null vector field is strictly non-negative for the admissible positive-non-affinity clock and matter satisfying the null energy condition. Strikingly, this local second law holds even when the boundary area is decreasing, disentangling thermodynamic irreversibility and the second law from standard area theorems. Our first-principles construction demonstrates that gravitational thermodynamics requires neither a stationary background, teleological event horizons, nor an exact entropy state function on individual codimension-1 spacelike slices of the null boundary (codimension two in spacetime).</p>

<p>We give a constructive criterion for local separation of first-order chiral systems coupling two complex doublets. <p>We carry out a systematic study of the impact of dynamical couplings of the vertices on the manifestations of the triangle singularity (TS) in the loop amplitude. Twelve combinations of external and internal spin-parity quantum numbers, covering the $PPS$, $VPP$, $VVP$, $VPA$, $VVV$, $VVA$, and $VVS$ effective vertices, are enumerated.
